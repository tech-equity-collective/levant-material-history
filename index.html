Levant Material History and Archaeogenomic Archive

This independent digital archive compiles peer-reviewed, empirical, and historical data to document the material realities and demographic continuity of the Southern Levant. Operating under rigorous evidentiary standards, this repository prioritizes primary physical and biological data over political and nationalist mythology.

The Archaeogenomics of Continuity: Paleogenomic Evidence of Indigenous Lineage in the Southern Levant

Abstract: Recent advancements in paleogenomics and ancient DNA (aDNA) provide an empirical, biometric framework for analyzing population continuity. Modern Palestinians derive approximately 75% to 88% of their ancestry from Bronze Age Levantine (Canaanite) populations, establishing a documented biological continuity that spans millennia. The 7th-century Islamic expansion was a cultural and linguistic shift, not a mass demographic replacement. Modern state immigration structures like the Law of Return create a profound contradiction by granting immediate citizenship to global converts while legally denying the right of return to biologically continuous indigenous inhabitants who hold documented land deeds.

The 1929 Buraq Uprising: Deconstructing Colonial Imbalances and Land Displacement

In August 1929, an uprising occurred across Mandatory Palestine. Within standard institutional histories, these events are frequently cataloged as unprovoked, ethnic or religious riots. Applying Parrino's protocol requires evaluating the underlying material realities and imperial power structures. In 1929, Palestine was under British military occupation, tasked with enforcing the Balfour Declaration against the explicit democratic will of the indigenous population.

Throughout the 1920s, Zionist land-buying funds, backed by foreign capital, purchased massive tracts of fertile agricultural land from absentee landlords, resulting in the immediate legal eviction of thousands of Palestinian tenant farmers (fellahin). Cut off from their traditional livelihoods, these populations faced economic ruin. While the flashpoint was triggered on August 15 by a provocative march of right-wing Zionist youths at the Western Wall, the resulting violence was a reaction to systemic displacement. Ultimately, the macro-apparatus of violence remained with the British state, whose military forces shot and killed the vast majority of the 116 Arab casualties to defend the imperial status quo.
